## Quick steps

1. Return to a lure tier and area where you can already kill catches consistently with your current equipment.
2. Use a suitable weapon so the catch does not escape or damage you heavily.
3. Add simple, repeatable killscore actions rather than forcing risky stunt chains.
4. If the grill is unlocked, follow the [cooking steps](/items/grilling-guide/#quick-steps): place one ordinary, low-value catch on it and remove it when cooked. Keep quest items off the grill. If it is still locked, use that same section for the unlock steps.
5. Check the displayed sale value before cooking more catches. The [cooking value and version notes](/items/grilling-guide/#cooking-has-three-different-uses) explain why an older multiplier should not be assumed for your version. Keep any cooked food you need for healing.
6. Hold each item you want to sell at least once, then sell it. If the shop refuses it, pick it up and retry; this is the sale prerequisite documented in Patch 1.0.11.
7. Set aside money for the next story item before optional purchases or gambling. Use gambling only when you can afford to lose the entire stake.

## Raise catch value before selling

The official Steam page confirms that trick shots increase money and gambling exists, but those two systems have different reliability. Killscore rewards skilled actions attached to a creature you already control. Gambling can return nothing useful. For progression, consistency beats the biggest possible screenshot.

Choose catches that match your current gun. A longer struggle can consume more ammunition and healing supplies. Once you unlock the grill on Island 3, cooking can support both selling and healing; check the displayed value in your version before committing a batch to sale.

Patch 1.0.11 changes one shop precondition: items need to have been held once before selling. If an item is present but the shop will not accept it, hold it once and return to the selling flow before assuming the save or vendor is broken. The announcement does not publish new sale prices, multipliers, or an hourly earning rate.

These steps are a repeatable spending-and-selling plan, not a timed route comparison. The sources reviewed for this page do not establish which route is fastest with a specified version, equipment, ammunition cost, and measured completion time.

## Why it may not work

- **A high multiplier reduces your profit:** difficult tricks can waste ammunition or let the creature escape. Use only bonuses you can reproduce.
- **Cooking produces a burnt item:** learn the grill timing on cheap catches before using a valuable one.
- **A new island feels less profitable:** stronger creatures require more damage and healing; return to the previous safe loop until upgraded.
- **Gambling emptied the upgrade fund:** it has no dependable return. Separate achievement attempts from story money.
- **A guide promises a fixed hourly rate:** route speed, weapon state, multiplayer, and patches make that figure unreliable.

## Solo and co-op notes

Solo players should favor repeatable catches and sell before a boss. In co-op, agree on who funds shared progression and who buys personal attachments. Do not assume another player’s gambling win will cover a required lure.

## What to do next

Spend the first stable surplus with [early upgrades](/items/early-upgrades/) or the broader [weapon progression guide](/items/weapon-progression/). Unlock and practice the [grilling steps](/items/grilling-guide/#quick-steps) before using high-value catches.
